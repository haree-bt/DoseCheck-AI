OVERVIEW:
Hypertension and cardiovascular therapies include calcium channel blockers (Amlodipine), ACE inhibitors (Lisinopril, Enalapril), angiotensin receptor blockers (Losartan), beta-blockers (Metoprolol), and statins (Atorvastatin, Simvastatin).

CRITICAL DRUG INTERACTIONS:
1. NSAIDs AND ANTIHYPERTENSIVE THERAPY:
- Common NSAIDs such as ibuprofen and naproxen cause systemic renal vasoconstriction and sodium/water retention.
- NSAIDs counteract and blunt the blood pressure-lowering effectiveness of amlodipine, lisinopril, and beta-blockers.
- Taking NSAIDs alongside ACE inhibitors (lisinopril) substantially increases the risk of acute kidney injury (nephrotoxicity), particularly in elderly or dehydrated individuals.

2. STATIN INTERACTIONS:
- Amlodipine increases systemic exposure to Simvastatin. When taken concurrently with amlodipine, the daily dose of simvastatin should not exceed 20 mg daily to prevent statin-induced rhabdomyolysis and myopathy.
- Paracetamol (acetaminophen) is generally safe to take with amlodipine and ACE inhibitors for mild to moderate pain relief.
