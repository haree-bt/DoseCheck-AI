OVERVIEW:
Acetaminophen (commonly known as Tylenol or Paracetamol) is an active ingredient in hundreds of over-the-counter (OTC) and prescription medicines used to relieve pain and reduce fever.

MAXIMUM DAILY DOSAGE LIMITS:
- For healthy adults and teenagers 12 years and older, the absolute maximum daily limit of acetaminophen is 4,000 milligrams (mg) in a 24-hour period.
- Many healthcare providers and manufacturers recommend an even lower daily limit of 3,000 mg per day to provide an extra safety buffer against unintentional overdose.
- Exceeding the maximum recommended daily dose can lead to severe, irreversible liver injury, liver failure, and death.

COMBINATION MEDICINE HAZARD:
- Acetaminophen is often combined with other active ingredients in multi-symptom cold, cough, flu, headache, allergy, and sleep-aid medicines (e.g., NyQuil, DayQuil, Excedrin, Percocet, Vicodin).
- Taking more than one medication containing acetaminophen at the same time is the leading cause of accidental acetaminophen overdose.
- Always check the "Drug Facts" label for the word "acetaminophen" or abbreviations such as "APAP". Never take two products containing acetaminophen simultaneously.

ALCOHOL WARNING:
- Patients who consume three or more alcoholic beverages every day must consult a doctor before using acetaminophen, as chronic alcohol use significantly multiplies the risk of severe liver toxicity.

SIGNS OF ACETAMINOPHEN OVERDOSE:
- Early symptoms may include nausea, vomiting, loss of appetite, sweating, extreme fatigue, and abdominal pain (especially in the upper right quadrant).
- In cases of suspected overdose, immediate medical intervention is critical even if initial symptoms appear mild.
